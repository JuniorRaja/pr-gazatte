# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Hiring managers and recruiters**: encountering the site after a referral or cold application. They've seen hundreds of portfolios; they're pattern-matching in the first 10 seconds. They should feel: *this person is distinctive and deliberate, not another template.*
- **Technical collaborators and peers**: engineers, designers, PMs who may work with Prasanna. They should feel: *this person has depth and craft; I'd enjoy working with them.*

Both audiences read the newspaper framing before they read any content. The aesthetic is the first impression.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Prasanna Rajendran, styled as a vintage digital broadsheet newspaper. Single scrolling page, 11 editorial sections. The design concept is the primary differentiator: the newspaper format itself signals editorial intelligence, deliberate craft, and a willingness to do the unexpected.

Success looks like: visitors remember *Prasanna*, not just "saw a cool portfolio." The site should be memorable enough to describe to someone else the next day.

## Brand Personality

Warm, curious, approachable, witty, and confident. The newspaper framing is not austere or ironic — it's genuine. There's a person behind it who enjoys the bit and commits to it fully. Dry humor is welcome; coldness is not.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dev portfolio**: hero section, skills cards grid, project tiles, contact form. Clean, forgettable, immediately forgettable after closing the tab.
- **AI-generated aesthetic**: gradient text, glassmorphic cards, identical feature cards on a pastel gradient, motion on everything. Looks assembled in 20 minutes from a template. The opposite of deliberate.

## Design Principles

1. **The medium is the message.** The newspaper format earns its keep by actively communicating: editorial judgment, sustained attention, a willingness to do the unexpected. If a section feels like it could live on any portfolio, it should be rethought.
2. **Warm precision.** Confident, opinionated choices without coldness. The vintage aesthetic is inviting, not museum-glass distant.
3. **Show, don't tell.** A developer building a site this polished demonstrates frontend taste without a bullet point that says "attention to detail." Every section should embody the skill it describes.
4. **Resist the swappable module.** No section should feel like it could be lifted out and placed in a generic portfolio. Each section has a distinct editorial voice.
5. **Committed to the bit.** Half-hearted vintage is worse than no vintage. The newspaper metaphors (bylines, datelines, column rules, section flags) should be thorough, not decorative sprinkles.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG requirement stated. Practical standard: WCAG AA contrast for body text, keyboard navigation for interactive elements, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for the ticker and page animations (already implemented in CSS).
